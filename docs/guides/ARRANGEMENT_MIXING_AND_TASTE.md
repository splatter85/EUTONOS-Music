# Arrangement, mixing, and taste

**Status:** Educational production practice and proposed acceptance process. No audio has been auditioned as part of writing this guide, and no automatic quality judgment is represented as a listening result.

**Edition:** 2026-11-03, America/Chicago.

A convincing musical system needs more than valid notes. It needs hierarchy, timing, contrast, sound selection, and judgment. This guide explains how to develop and evaluate those qualities, from a short authored passage to an adaptive playback session.

Use [Music foundations](MUSIC_FOUNDATIONS.md) for basic theory, [Genre recipes](GENRE_RECIPES.md) for study directions, and [Recipe authoring](RECIPE_AUTHORING.md) for the rule and metadata representation.

## 1. Give the listener a focal point

At any moment, decide what matters most. It might be a voice, a bass groove, a recurring synth figure, the conversation between drums, or the changing texture of an environment.

Assign supporting roles around that focus:

| Role | Musical responsibility | Useful restraint |
|---|---|---|
| Foreground | Carry the main identity or sentence | Let it finish and breathe |
| Foundation | Establish pulse and harmonic weight | Stay predictable when other parts become active |
| Support | Make harmony and atmosphere legible | Leave the foreground's register or rhythmic space available |
| Response | Answer or develop an idea | Enter where the main idea leaves an opening |
| Detail | Add a distinctive small event | Avoid demanding attention on every repeat |

These are proposed production roles, not a rule that every composition requires five tracks. One instrument may perform several jobs. Ambient or contrapuntal music may deliberately distribute attention.

Try a reduction pass: mute every part except the foreground and foundation. Add each remaining part one at a time. Ask what becomes clearer, more expressive, or more interesting. If the answer is only “there is more sound,” reconsider the part.

## 2. Arrange at several time scales

A good short loop and a good long passage solve different problems.

| Time scale | Decisions to make |
|---|---|
| Individual event | Attack, duration, emphasis, articulation |
| Beat or bar | Groove, space, response, accent |
| Phrase | Statement, development, ending |
| Section | Identity, contrast, tension, return |
| Whole piece | Overall direction and proportion |
| Playback session | Rotation, continuity, listener preference, repetition fatigue |

A working soundtrack also needs stable passages where it can remain without sounding stuck. Write a holding section that contains small internal changes, an optional development, and a musically credible exit. A holding section should have a reason to continue even when no new work activity occurs.

Dennis DeSantis's producer-oriented book presents constraints as a practical way to move from an open-ended possibility space toward a finished result. The exercises here use that principle: select a limited set of decisions, finish a passage, then evaluate it. [A1]

### A 64-bar arrangement exercise

Use one main motif, one response, and a consistent palette. The following is a proposed plan, not a required song form.

| Bars | Purpose | Arrangement move | What must remain recognizable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–8 | Introduce | Pulse, bass fragment, a hint of the motif | Groove and sound identity |
| 9–24 | Establish | Complete motif and harmonic support | Main phrase |
| 25–32 | Create space | Remove a familiar layer; expose a response | Harmonic direction |
| 33–48 | Return with development | Restore the main idea with one meaningful addition | Motif's rhythmic fingerprint |
| 49–56 | Ease back | Reduce density and shorten foreground activity | Useful pulse or texture |
| 57–64 | Land or continue | Conclude, or use an authored bridge to a holding section | Clear musical intention |

For each section, state one reason it exists. Then perform the arrangement once without changes. Take notes after listening rather than stopping at every imperfection. This makes proportion easier to judge.

## 3. Create energy without treating loudness as the whole answer

Energy can change through several independent musical properties:

- Rhythmic activity and accent strength.
- Harmonic movement and unresolved tension.
- Register and brightness.
- Articulation, attack, and sustain.
- Number and prominence of foreground parts.
- Spatial closeness or distance.
- Contrast with what immediately preceded the passage.

A denser section can stay at a similar overall listening level. A quieter section can remain tense. A thinking passage can become more harmonically interesting without becoming faster.

Make three versions of the same phrase. Keep tempo and master level fixed. In the first, change only percussion density. In the second, change only articulation and timbre. In the third, change only harmonic rhythm. Record which changes feel useful and which feel distracting. This is an audition exercise; its outcome depends on the music and listener.

For adaptive playback, use a **mix plan** and an **arrangement plan** together. Adding two stems changes both density and level. A control labeled “energy” should therefore describe a curated set of coordinated decisions rather than independently maximizing every track.

## 4. Musical transitions need permission from the music

A desired state can change immediately while the arrangement changes at a suitable musical point. This separation gives a phrase time to finish. Immediate stop, mute, or explicit listener commands remain direct controls; a musical waiting rule should not prevent them.

For every proposed **transition edge**, review:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Meter and timing | Do the grids align, or is a deliberate bridge required? |
| Harmonic phase | Are the active chords compatible at the actual overlap? |
| Phrase | Does either part need to finish a thought? |
| Foreground | Will two hooks or voices compete? |
| Sound and level | Does the incoming palette create an abrupt change in weight or brightness? |
| Tails | What continues ringing after the source stops? |
| Meaning | Would a lyric or recognizable statement be interrupted? |

A shared key label is insufficient. Two assets in A minor may be on different chords, and their held notes may conflict. Two passages at the same BPM may use different swing, half-time interpretation, or phrase placement.

Write several transition types: a layer exchange within one arrangement, a phrase-boundary handoff, a percussion bridge, a deliberate breakdown, or a complete ending followed by another opening. Accept only the specific edges that have been tested. The [library and transitions design](../LIBRARY_AND_TRANSITIONS.md) records the broader product approach.

## 5. Mix in an order that exposes the actual problem

Begin with sound selection and arrangement. A competing part may need fewer notes or a different register before it needs more processing.

A practical sequence:

1. Set a comfortable monitoring level and disable unnecessary loudness processing.
2. Balance the main roles using faders.
3. Adjust durations and remove avoidable overlaps.
4. Choose panning and spatial relationships.
5. Use equalization where it serves a named problem or color.
6. Use dynamics processing for a defined purpose.
7. Restore creative effects and evaluate them in context.
8. Compare the final arrangement against an appropriate reference.

Each change should have an intended result. “The lead gets clearer during the dense section” is a useful target. “Every track needs compression” is not a diagnosis.

### Diagnose by listening relationship

| Observation | First experiment | Evidence to compare |
|---|---|---|
| Kick loses definition when bass enters | Shorten or move one bass event | Same bar with and without the change |
| Lead feels buried | Reduce a competing part before boosting the lead | Foreground intelligibility at matched overall level |
| Chords sound muddy | Change voicing or remove low duplication | Same harmony in two registers |
| Hats feel tiring | Reduce their level or choose a softer attack | A longer passage, not one isolated hit |
| Mix feels small | Create contrast and depth before widening everything | Sparse and full sections together |
| Dense section sounds flat | Restore transient or arrangement contrast | Loudness-matched before/after |

Equalization shapes spectral balance. Compression changes level over time according to its detector and settings. Neither fixes every musical conflict. Evaluate the full context after an isolated adjustment.

## 6. Treat kick and bass as an arrangement

Low-end cooperation begins with rhythm, register, and envelopes. Choose which sound supplies the lowest sustained weight and which supplies the transient. That relationship can vary by recipe.

Try these alternatives before committing to processing:

- A short kick with a bass note that begins after it.
- A more sustained kick with shorter, less frequent bass notes.
- A bass part that shares selected kick accents but leaves others exposed.
- A unified low-end gesture that intentionally performs both jobs.

External sidechain compression allows one signal to control the level reduction of another. FabFilter's explanation distinguishes the detector path from the audio being heard and demonstrates creative uses. [A2] Use it when that behavior suits the music. Tune its timing with the actual kick and bass; a preset value cannot know their envelopes.

A stem rendered with ducking contains that performance. To reproduce a reference that used ducking, a dry bass stem requires the corresponding relationship to be recreated during playback. Record which one you are supplying.

Also review phase and polarity with the actual sounds. Two apparently similar low-frequency layers can reinforce or reduce each other differently as their waveforms interact. Audition the combined result across the intended pitch range; a single successful note is not enough evidence for the whole bass part.

## 7. Space, width, and dynamics

Use space to communicate relationships. A close dry part and a distant reverberant part can be easier to follow than five equally wide, equally reverberant parts. Decide whether the reverb describes a shared room or a deliberate surreal effect.

Long releases, delays, and reverbs continue into later harmony. Include them in the composition review. Shortening a pad release may improve a chord transition more than changing the chord.

Check the mix in mono as well as stereo. Stereo processing can change balance or cancel material when channels are combined; iZotope's technical article demonstrates the issue and why it matters for reproduction. [A3] Keep essential identity audible in the intended playback conditions. This is a compatibility check, not a rule that all music should have the same width.

Keep distinct records for:

| Measurement | What it helps reveal |
|---|---|
| Sample peak | Highest stored sample amplitude |
| True-peak estimate | Possible reconstructed peaks between samples |
| Loudness over time | Overall level behavior across passages |
| Crest or dynamic behavior | Relationship between peaks and sustained energy |
| Spectral balance | Which frequency regions dominate |
| Stereo/mono comparison | Whether important material survives channel combination |

Meters support a judgment; they do not establish groove, emotional direction, or taste. Choose delivery targets for the actual format and use case. A single LUFS or headroom number should not become a universal aesthetic requirement.

FL Studio's documentation distinguishes internal floating-point headroom from output and rendered-file clipping. Check the relevant output stage and plugins rather than assuming an internal meter tells the entire story. [A4]

## 8. Use references as a controlled comparison

Choose two or three authorized reference tracks or performances relevant to the intended result. Each can answer a different question: low-end relationship, percussion detail, harmonic pacing, or spatial perspective.

Bring their listening level close to the work in progress and compare similar sections. Otherwise the louder or denser passage can bias the judgment. iZotope's producer tutorial explicitly recommends level matching and examining relationships among parts. [A5]

Use a short written comparison:

| Question | Observation | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| What is foremost? | Our pad covers the motif's middle notes | Reduce pad activity during the motif |
| How does bass relate to kick? | Our bass sustains across every kick | Test shorter notes and a different entry |
| Where does the reference rest? | Its answer leaves a full beat open | Remove a decorative response |
| How does the return feel stronger? | It restores a missing register | Try an octave change before adding a track |

Study decisions and relationships. Write an original melody, original lyrics, and an original arrangement. A reference does not need to become a template that duplicates another person's expression.

## 9. Prepare stems that can actually recombine

A stem bundle is a coordinated musical object. Exporting several files does not by itself prove that they can be freely combined.

At minimum, preserve:

- A common timeline origin, tempo interpretation, meter, and phrase length.
- The harmony timeline, key/tuning context, and actual chord positions.
- Role names and required/optional relationships.
- Dry/wet status, shared effects, and sidechain dependencies.
- Loop boundary, pickup, release-tail, and safe-exit information.
- Render format, source project, instrument versions, and level plan.
- Which combinations and transition edges were auditioned.

Shared nonlinear processing matters. In general, processing the full sum through a compressor or saturator does not equal processing each part separately and adding the results. If the intended sound depends on a shared bus, preserve that dependency, render suitable grouped stems, or accept a different playback mix deliberately.

Image-Line documents that Split Mixer Tracks and Playlist-track export have different behavior, including master-effect handling. Use the [FL Studio workflow](FL_STUDIO_WORKFLOW.md) to select the correct export method and avoid assuming that a split export reconstructs the master automatically. [A6]

**Recombination exercise:** Reimport the exported bundle into a clean session, align the common start, and follow the documented mix plan. Compare with the approved reference render. Investigate differences that matter. A null test is useful only when the processing is expected to reconstruct exactly; random or nonlinear processing can prevent exact cancellation.

## 10. Taste is a review process

Good judgment becomes easier to apply when feedback is specific and alternatives are comparable.

Use three categories:

| Category | Examples | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Technical defect | Click, missing sample, wrong timing unit, unexpected clipping | Fix or reject |
| Musical mismatch | Bass contradicts the intended harmony; phrase ends awkwardly | Revise the responsible musical choice |
| Taste preference | Warmer palette, fewer fills, more melodic development | Record the listener's preference and compare alternatives |

Do not turn all preferences into universal rules. A deliberately abrasive industrial recipe and a soft chamber passage need different criteria.

### A small review rubric

Score each item from 1 to 5 only after listening, with a short observation. Scores organize discussion; they are not an objective measure of musical worth.

- **Identity:** Can the main idea be recognized?
- **Groove or pacing:** Does time feel intentional?
- **Role cooperation:** Can the important parts be followed?
- **Development:** Does the passage change with purpose?
- **Sound:** Do the chosen instruments serve the idea?
- **Comfort for the intended context:** Is the experience welcome at the selected intensity?
- **Repeat value:** What becomes obvious or tiring after several returns?

A high total cannot erase a critical defect. Keep the audio, version, listening conditions, listener, and date with the review.

### Controlled A/B revision

Freeze the seed, arrangement, and palette. Change one property, such as bass duration or the lead's final phrase. Render both versions at comparable levels. Listen in an order that does not always favor the new version. Record the choice and its reason.

If neither version solves the problem, reconsider the diagnosis. Repeatedly generating new seeds can obscure a structural weakness in the recipe.

## 11. Test the longer experience

Review a short passage first. Then listen to a longer arrangement and finally a realistic playback session. Extend testing to resolve an actual uncertainty: repetition, transition quality, density drift, or listener control.

Record concrete events:

| Time | Observation | Likely owner |
|---|---|---|
| 04:20 | Same fill returns too conspicuously | Recipe variation policy |
| 09:10 | Incoming pad overlaps an incompatible chord | Transition edge |
| 16:40 | Quiet state still has a demanding foreground | Arrangement and mode mapping |
| 23:05 | Bass changes level when a new variant enters | Palette or mix plan |
| 31:00 | Listener wants to stay in the current groove | Playback preference/control |

A successful short render does not establish a successful extended session. Equally, a session need not visit every eligible asset to be varied. Curate around the listener's preferences and the musical route available.

The project's long-duration goal belongs to [Product goal](../PRODUCT_GOAL.md) and [Delivery plan](../DELIVERY_PLAN.md). This guide supplies a method for gathering musical evidence toward it.

## 12. Completion criteria for a musical package

A proposed package is ready for integration review when it has:

1. An authored identity and an approved reference render.
2. A clear arrangement plan and mix plan.
3. Tested intensity variants or an honest fixed-intensity label.
4. A coherent stem bundle or documented self-contained asset.
5. Specific accepted transitions, with limitations.
6. A review record describing actual listening and unresolved issues.
7. Reproducible source information and appropriate asset permissions.

“Rendered successfully” and “musically accepted” should be separate states. Both matter, and neither should be inferred from the other.
